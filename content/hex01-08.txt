@1 | The Creative | Heaven, Force
key: creative power; initiative; persistence; leadership; timing
core: a time of strong creative energy, when steady, principled effort can start something important
now: You are in a period of strong, active energy. Something wants to begin, grow or take shape through you, and you have more power to influence events than you may think. The Creative is pure strength: heaven moving without pause. Its traditional words promise great success, but only to effort that stays correct and persistent. The key is not force for its own sake but strength that keeps going, day after day, in the right direction. Notice where your energy naturally wants to go, and ask whether your ambition serves something worthwhile.
dir: The situation may be moving toward more initiative and momentum. Energy that was held back is ready to act, and steady, principled effort is likely to carry further than hesitation.
do: Commit to a clear direction and work at it consistently; Match your timing to the moment, waiting when the time is early and acting when it is ripe; Lead by example rather than by pressure
avoid: Pushing past healthy limits out of pride; Using strength to dominate other people
love: Bring warmth and initiative, but let your partner meet you halfway; strength in a relationship means reliability, not control.
work: A good time to start or lead a project. Set a clear goal, keep a steady pace and earn trust through consistent results.
self: Your energy is high. Channel it into a discipline you can sustain, rather than bursts of effort followed by exhaustion.
jung: The Creative can mirror the archetype of the active, conscious will, sometimes pictured as the hero or the sun. Ask whether you are identifying too strongly with your own power. Jung warned that inflation, the sense of being larger than life, often comes just before a fall, which the sixth line's arrogant dragon also describes.
ex: Someone starting a business, a training plan or a creative project who needs to keep showing up every day, while knowing when to pause.
1: The dragon is still hidden under the water. Your abilities are real but not yet ready to be shown. This is a time for preparation, not action. Keep developing quietly and do not force an early start.
2: The dragon appears in the field. Your potential is becoming visible. Seek out people with experience and influence; a good mentor or ally can help you grow now.
3: You are working hard all day and still alert at night. The position is demanding and carries some danger, but steady care keeps you from mistakes. Stay diligent without letting worry consume you.
4: The dragon seems ready to leap, yet is still in the deep water. You stand at a threshold and can choose to rise or wait. Either choice can be right if it is made honestly; there is no blame.
5: The dragon flies in the sky. You are at the height of your power and influence. Use it well, and stay connected with capable and wise people.
6: The dragon has gone too high and exceeded its limits. Pride and overreach lead to regret. Step back before strength turns into arrogance.
7: When all six lines change, a host of dragons appears with no one at their head. Strength that does not insist on being first brings good fortune: lead through cooperation, not domination.

@2 | The Receptive | Earth, Field
key: receptivity; support; patience; devotion; following
core: a time to be receptive, supportive and patient, letting things grow rather than forcing them
now: This is a time for receptivity rather than initiative. Like the earth that receives seed and quietly supports all growth, you may achieve the most by responding, supporting and following a sound lead rather than trying to control the situation. Legge's text says that if the superior person takes the lead he goes astray, but if he follows he finds his way. Receptive does not mean passive: it means steady, dependable and open, like the "firmness of a mare" that keeps going without fighting the rider. Look for what your situation needs from you, then give it patiently.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a quieter, more receptive phase, where patience, cooperation and support achieve more than personal initiative.
do: Listen carefully and respond to what is actually needed; Support people and projects that deserve it; Be patient and steady; let results grow in their own time
avoid: Forcing your own agenda; Mistaking quiet strength for weakness
love: Listen more than you speak and show care through consistent, practical support. Do not try to steer everything.
work: Support a strong leader or plan rather than competing for control. Thorough, reliable work will be noticed.
self: Practise acceptance. Not everything needs to be fixed by effort; some things need room to grow.
jung: The Receptive is often linked with the archetypal feminine, the mother and the earth: the capacity to receive, hold and nourish. Psychologically it can invite you to listen to the unconscious rather than to command it. Dreams, feelings and quiet intuitions may carry guidance now.
ex: A new team member who learns the system before proposing changes, or a parent who supports a child's interests instead of directing them.
1: Frost underfoot means hard ice will follow. Small signs show where things are heading. Pay attention to early warnings and deal with problems while they are still small.
2: Straight, square and great: honest, natural action succeeds without strain. You do not need elaborate effort; acting from your true nature is enough.
3: You keep your talents hidden but remain steady. If you serve a larger cause, do not claim credit; your work will still bring things to a good end.
4: A tied-up sack: be discreet and keep your counsel. Staying quiet earns neither blame nor praise, and that is what this moment requires.
5: The yellow lower garment, a symbol of modesty and the middle way. Quiet dignity and reliable service bring great good fortune.
6: Dragons fight in the wild and their blood is spilled. When the receptive tries to compete with the creative, both are hurt. Avoid a power struggle that has gone too far.
7: When all six lines change, the text advises lasting firmness and correctness. Receptivity should be steady and sincere over the long term; constancy turns yielding into strength.

@3 | Difficulty at the Beginning | Sprouting
key: new beginnings; confusion; growing pains; help; patience
core: the messy, difficult start of something new, which succeeds with patience, structure and help
now: You may be at the beginning of something new, and beginnings are rarely tidy. The hexagram shows a seedling pushing up through hard ground, or clouds and thunder before a storm clears the air. There is great potential and real chaos at the same time. Legge's text promises great progress, but warns against rushing forward and advises "appointing feudal princes" — finding helpers and creating structure. Confusion now is a natural stage of growth, not a sign of failure. Organize what you can, ask for help and do not give up.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a new beginning, with the usual confusion of early growth. It will need patience, structure and support from others.
do: Break the situation into small, manageable steps; Ask for help and gather allies; Persist calmly through the early confusion
avoid: Rash, premature moves; Giving up because the start is difficult
love: A new or renewed relationship may feel uncertain. Go slowly, communicate clearly and do not judge its future by its awkward start.
work: New ventures face growing pains. Put basic structures in place and recruit good helpers before expanding.
self: Accept that learning something new feels clumsy. Confusion is the doorway to growth.
jung: Psychologically this can describe the birth of something new from the unconscious, a new attitude or creative idea struggling into consciousness. Such births are often accompanied by anxiety and disorder. Hold the tension rather than suppressing the new impulse.
ex: The first months of a new job, a move to a new city, or launching a project before routines are established.
1: Difficulty in moving forward. Stay firm and correct, and gather helpers. Hesitation at this stage is wise; build your base before you advance.
2: Progress stalls and you feel turned back. An offer comes that is not what it seems, or not yet right. Stay true to yourself; the right union will come in time, even if it takes long.
3: Chasing a deer without a guide leads only deeper into the forest. Going on alone and unprepared will bring regret. The wise choice is to stop.
4: Now is the time to seek a partner or helper. Accept the connection that is offered; moving forward together brings good fortune.
5: You have resources to give, but conditions limit how much you can do. Be careful in small matters; trying to accomplish too much at once would be unwise.
6: Tears of blood flow as the horses turn back. Despair at the difficulty of the beginning. Grief should not last forever; change your approach rather than staying stuck.

@4 | Youthful Folly | Inexperience
key: learning; inexperience; teaching; humility; guidance
core: a time of inexperience, when sincere learning and good guidance matter more than pretending to know
now: This hexagram is about learning. Someone is inexperienced, perhaps you, perhaps someone you are guiding. The image is a spring flowing at the foot of a mountain: fresh water that does not yet know its way. The traditional text says the teacher does not seek the student; the student must come sincerely. It also notes that asking the same question again and again is disrespectful — a pointed reminder about re-consulting the oracle. Approach the situation with an open, teachable mind, and be honest about what you do not yet know.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a learning phase. You may need to seek knowledge, accept guidance or admit what you do not yet understand.
do: Ask sincerely for guidance and then act on it; Admit what you do not know; Learn step by step, with patience
avoid: Asking the same question repeatedly hoping for a different answer; Pretending to know more than you do
love: Be honest about your inexperience or confusion. Learn about your partner rather than assuming you already understand.
work: Seek training or a mentor. If you are leading, teach patiently but set clear boundaries.
self: Curiosity is your best ally now. Treat confusion as the beginning of understanding.
jung: Youthful Folly can mirror the archetype of the puer or child: fresh, open but naive. It may also point to the relationship between ego and inner wisdom. The ego must approach the deeper Self sincerely, not demand instant answers.
ex: A student facing a difficult subject, a new parent learning on the job, or someone repeatedly asking for advice they never follow.
1: To dispel ignorance, discipline can help, but punishment should not become a habit. Use firm structure to start learning, then loosen it.
2: Bear patiently with the inexperienced, and even accept help from unexpected people. Like a son able to carry the family, you can take responsibility well.
3: Do not commit to someone who abandons themselves at the sight of wealth or advantage. Avoid relationships or choices based on mere attraction to power or money.
4: Bound by ignorance and isolation. Cutting yourself off from good guidance leads to regret. Seek out people who can help.
5: The innocent, childlike learner meets good fortune. Humility and openness make you easy to teach.
6: Correcting folly: discipline is needed, but do not attack. Protect against harm rather than inflicting it.

@5 | Waiting | Nourishment while waiting
key: patience; timing; preparation; confidence; nourishment
core: a time of waiting for the right moment, using the pause to prepare and stay nourished
now: You are being asked to wait — not anxiously, but with confidence and readiness. The image is clouds rising in the sky: rain will come, but it cannot be forced. The traditional text links sincerity with brilliant success and says it will be advantageous to cross the great water — that is, to undertake something important, once the time is right. Waiting here is active. Take care of your health and relationships, gather information and prepare, so that when the moment arrives you are ready to move.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a period of waiting, when the most effective thing is to prepare calmly and trust that the right time will come.
do: Use the waiting time to prepare and gather strength; Keep up good habits and look after your wellbeing; Stay sincere and confident
avoid: Forcing a decision before conditions are ready; Letting worry turn waiting into suffering
love: Do not push for answers or commitments right now. Enjoy what is good in the present and let trust develop.
work: Prepare thoroughly, but wait for the right opening rather than forcing a launch or confrontation.
self: Practise patience as a skill. Rest, nourishment and calm are productive now.
jung: Waiting invites you to tolerate uncertainty without escaping into anxious action. Psychologically, insight often matures beneath the surface before it can be acted on. The ego's impatience is set against the slower rhythm of the unconscious.
ex: Waiting for exam results, a job offer or a medical recovery, and choosing to rest and prepare instead of worrying.
1: Waiting in the open meadow, far from danger. Keep up your normal routine and remain steady; there is no mistake.
2: Waiting on the sand, closer to trouble. There may be some gossip or criticism, but in the end things turn out well.
3: Waiting in the mud invites trouble. You have moved too close to danger. Be careful and avoid making things worse.
4: Waiting in blood: you are in a dangerous situation. Stay calm and get out of the hole; you will escape.
5: Waiting with wine and food. Even in difficult times, enjoy the nourishment available and keep steady. Firm correctness brings good fortune.
6: You fall into the pit, but three unexpected guests arrive. Receive them respectfully, even if they are not what you expected; help can come from surprising sources.

@6 | Conflict | Contention
key: conflict; dispute; fairness; compromise; caution
core: a conflict in which fairness, restraint and impartial help matter more than winning
now: This hexagram shows a conflict: heaven rising upward and water flowing down, moving apart. You may be in a dispute, or feel inner tension between what you want and what is possible. The traditional text says that even if you are sincere and in the right, you meet obstruction. Stopping halfway brings good fortune; pushing the conflict to the bitter end brings misfortune. Seeking a wise mediator helps. Before acting, ask whether you are clear about your goal, and whether winning would really be worth the cost.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a point of tension or disagreement. It will be important to handle conflict fairly and to know when to stop.
do: Clarify the facts and your real goals before arguing; Seek a fair mediator or neutral advice; Be willing to compromise halfway
avoid: Fighting to the bitter end; Starting major new ventures while a dispute is unresolved
love: Do not try to win the argument at all costs. Look for what each of you really needs and accept a fair middle ground.
work: Resolve disputes through clear agreements and neutral help. Get commitments in writing before starting new projects.
self: Inner conflict may mean two of your values are pulling against each other. Name both sides honestly before choosing.
jung: Conflict can represent a clash between opposites within the psyche, such as ego and shadow or duty and desire. Jung believed that holding the tension of opposites, rather than letting one side simply win, can lead to a new third position.
ex: A dispute with a neighbour, a business partner or a family member, where a fair compromise is wiser than a long legal battle.
1: Do not drag out the conflict. There may be a little criticism, but ending it early brings good fortune.
2: You cannot win this fight. Retreat home and keep quiet; the people around you will be spared harm.
3: Live on what you have honestly earned and stay correct. Danger exists, but you will come through. If you serve someone else, do not claim the success.
4: You cannot win, so you turn back and accept what is right. Changing your attitude and settling down brings good fortune.
5: Bringing your case before a fair and just authority. Contention handled with integrity brings great good fortune.
6: Even if you win honors through fighting, they may be taken away three times in a single morning. Victory gained through conflict is unstable.

@7 | The Army | Discipline, leadership
key: organization; discipline; leadership; responsibility; collective effort
core: a time for disciplined, organized collective effort under responsible leadership
now: The Army is about organized collective effort. The image is water hidden in the earth: a great force held within an orderly structure. Whatever you face may require discipline, coordination and a leader whom people trust — perhaps you. The traditional text says success depends on a mature, experienced leader and on a just cause. Clear roles, clear rules and a worthy purpose are essential. This is not about aggression; it is about bringing scattered energies under responsible direction.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a need for organization and discipline. Collective efforts will need clear leadership and a just purpose.
do: Define clear roles and responsibilities; Choose experienced leaders and trust them; Keep your purpose just and your methods disciplined
avoid: Disorder and divided command; Giving responsibility to inexperienced or self-serving people
love: Shared goals and fair roles strengthen a relationship. Work as a team, not as competitors.
work: Organize the team, clarify who decides what, and keep standards high. Discipline now prevents chaos later.
self: Bring order to your habits and priorities. Inner discipline gives your energy a direction.
jung: The Army can symbolize the organization of many inner drives under a central purpose. Psychologically, the question is who is in command: a mature, integrated center, or a fragmentary complex that has taken over.
ex: Organizing a community project, leading a sports team, or coordinating family members during a crisis.
1: An army must set out in proper order. Without discipline, even a good cause will end badly.
2: The leader is in the midst of the army, trusted and supported. There is good fortune; recognition comes three times.
3: The army carries corpses in its wagons. Divided leadership or incompetence brings misfortune.
4: The army retreats. Withdrawing at the right time is no mistake.
5: There is game in the field; it is right to act against it. Let the experienced elder lead; if inexperienced people take charge, misfortune follows.
6: The great ruler gives out rewards and founds states. Do not give power to petty or unworthy people, even after success.

@8 | Holding Together | Union
key: union; belonging; loyalty; community; trust
core: a time of joining together around a trustworthy center, where sincerity and good timing build lasting bonds
now: Holding Together is about union. Water on the earth flows together naturally, finding its level and joining with other water. You may be seeking or forming connections — with a group, a partner, a community or a cause. The traditional text advises you to examine yourself: are you worthy to be a center that others gather around, or are you joining the right center? It also warns that those who hesitate too long arrive late and miss out. Sincere, timely commitment brings good fortune.
dir: The situation may be moving toward union and belonging. It will be important to join the right people sincerely and without hesitation.
do: Examine whether your commitments are sincere and well-chosen; Join or build a community around shared values; Act in good time rather than waiting until the chance is gone
avoid: Joining the wrong group out of loneliness; Hesitating so long that the opportunity passes
love: Commitment and sincerity matter now. If the relationship is right, show it; if you are unsure, look honestly at why.
work: Build alliances and partnerships based on trust. A good team leader draws people together naturally.
self: Ask where you truly belong. Your loyalties shape who you become.
jung: Holding Together reflects the human need for belonging and the psychological task of finding a central value around which the personality can unite. Jung called this center the Self.
ex: Joining a club, committing to a partnership, or bringing a divided team together around a shared goal.
1: Hold to others with sincerity, as full as an earthen jug. Sincerity attracts unexpected good fortune.
2: Union comes from within. Stay true to your inner commitments; firmness brings good fortune.
3: You are seeking union with the wrong people. Be careful of associations that do not share your values.
4: Show your loyalty openly to someone worthy. Firm, outward commitment brings good fortune.
5: The king hunts on three sides and lets the game escape on the fourth. Union is offered freely, not forced. Let people come of their own accord; this brings good fortune.
6: Seeking union without a head, too late, without a true beginning. This leads to misfortune.
