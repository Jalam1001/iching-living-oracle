@17 | Following | Adapting
key: following; adaptability; trust; responsiveness; leadership by example
core: a time to follow wisely, adapting to circumstances and to worthy leadership without losing your principles
now: Thunder rests within the lake: energy adapts to the season. Following describes a time when the right response is to adapt and follow — whether that means following a worthy leader, a natural rhythm or the needs of the moment. The text promises supreme success when following remains correct. Good following is not blind obedience; it is choosing what deserves to be followed. Equally, if you lead, people will follow you willingly only if you first serve their needs.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a phase of adaptation, when flexibility and wise choices about whom or what to follow are important.
do: Adapt to changing circumstances; Choose carefully what and whom you follow; Rest and recover when the time calls for it
avoid: Following blindly or for selfish gain; Clinging rigidly to old plans
love: Be willing to adapt to your partner's needs and rhythms, while staying true to yourself.
work: Follow good leadership or adapt to market conditions. Leading by serving others earns loyalty.
self: Let go of rigidity. Flexibility can be a sign of strength.
jung: Following may describe the ego's willingness to follow the guidance of the deeper Self, which Jung saw as the center and guide of psychological development. It also raises the question of projection: whom do we follow, and why?
ex: Joining a respected mentor's project, adapting to a new workplace culture, or responding to a child's changing needs.
1: The standard changes. Go out of your own door to mix with others; achievements come.
2: Clinging to the little boy, you lose the strong man. Do not attach yourself to the wrong person or the lesser option.
3: Clinging to the strong man, you let go of the little boy. Following what is worthy means giving something up. Stay firm.
4: Following brings success, but persistence in this brings misfortune if motives are selfish. Sincerity and clarity remove blame.
5: Sincere in pursuing the good. Good fortune.
6: Firmly bound in loyalty. The king offers to the western mountain. Deep commitment that endures.

@18 | Work on What Has Been Spoiled | Repairing Decay
key: repair; renewal; responsibility; family patterns; correction
core: a time to repair what has decayed, especially problems inherited from the past
now: Wind blows low beneath the mountain, and things become stagnant. The character for this hexagram shows worms in a bowl: something has decayed through neglect. The text says this can lead to great success and advises reflecting on what came three days before and planning what comes three days after — understand the causes, then plan carefully. You may be dealing with problems inherited from the past, perhaps family patterns, old habits or neglected obligations. Repair is possible, but it requires honesty and effort.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a time of repair and renewal. Old problems or neglect will need to be faced honestly.
do: Identify the root causes of problems; Take responsibility for repair; Plan carefully before and after acting
avoid: Ignoring problems that keep returning; Blaming others without taking action
love: Address old wounds or recurring patterns honestly. Repair trust step by step.
work: Fix underlying problems rather than covering them up. Restructure what is not working.
self: Examine inherited habits and beliefs. Decide which to keep and which to change.
jung: Work on What Has Been Spoiled can point to inherited psychological patterns, family complexes passed from parents to children. Becoming conscious of them is the first step to breaking their hold.
ex: Repairing a neglected house, reforming a struggling organization, or healing family relationships after years of tension.
1: Setting right what the father spoiled. If the son is capable, the father is free of blame. Danger exists, but good fortune comes in the end.
2: Setting right what the mother spoiled. Be gentle; do not be too rigid.
3: Setting right what the father spoiled. There may be slight regret, but no great blame.
4: Tolerating what the father spoiled. Continuing to look away leads to humiliation.
5: Setting right what the father spoiled earns praise. Repairing with good help brings honour.
6: Not serving kings and princes, setting higher goals. Some people step back from public affairs to cultivate inner values.

@19 | Approach | Drawing Near
key: approach; growth; leadership; opportunity; care
core: a time of approach and growing influence, when kindness and teaching strengthen relationships before the season turns
now: The earth stands above the lake, close and caring. Approach describes a time when something good draws near: opportunities, influence or relationships grow. The text promises great success but warns that "in the eighth month there will be misfortune" — favourable seasons do not last forever. Use this time well. Approach people with warmth and patience, teach and support others, and prepare for the change that will eventually come.
dir: The situation may be moving toward growth and new opportunities. Use the favourable time wisely and remember that it will not last forever.
do: Make the most of favourable conditions; Approach people with kindness and patience; Prepare for future change
avoid: Complacency; Assuming good times will last forever
love: Draw closer with warmth and care. A good time to deepen connection.
work: Influence is growing. Mentor others and expand thoughtfully.
self: You are growing. Use this momentum to build lasting habits.
jung: Approach may describe the conscious mind drawing near to new content from the unconscious. It is a time of opening and receptivity, when insights and new attitudes can be accepted.
ex: A new supervisor getting to know the team, or a growing friendship that is deepening.
1: Joint approach. Approaching together with others; firmness brings good fortune.
2: Joint approach. Good fortune; everything furthers.
3: Comfortable approach. Easy, careless approach brings nothing good. If you recognize this and change, there is no blame.
4: Complete approach. Fully engaged, without blame.
5: Wise approach, befitting a great ruler. Good fortune.
6: Great-hearted approach. Generosity and sincerity bring good fortune and no blame.

@20 | Contemplation | View
key: contemplation; observation; perspective; example; reflection
core: a time of contemplation and observation, when understanding and good example matter more than action
now: Wind moves over the earth, reaching everything. Contemplation shows a tower from which one sees widely, and also a person who is seen by others. The text describes the moment of a ritual when the worshipper has washed their hands but not yet made the offering: full of sincere attention. This is a time to observe, reflect and understand before acting. Your example and attitude influence others more than your words.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a phase of reflection and observation. Understanding the larger picture will be more important than immediate action.
do: Observe carefully before acting; Reflect on your own conduct and its effects; Set a good example
avoid: Acting without understanding; Superficial judgement
love: Observe and understand each other's needs. Quiet attention can deepen intimacy.
work: Step back to see the big picture. Leaders influence by example.
self: A good time for meditation, journaling or reflection.
jung: Contemplation suggests turning attention inward to observe the movements of the psyche. Jung's method of active imagination involves this kind of patient, attentive observation of inner images.
ex: A manager observing how a team works before making changes, or a person reflecting on their life before a major decision.
1: Boyish contemplation. Shallow viewing is acceptable for ordinary people, but not for those who should know better.
2: Contemplation through a crack in the door. A narrow view; acceptable only in limited contexts.
3: Contemplating your own life to decide whether to advance or retreat. Self-examination guides your next step.
4: Contemplating the light of the kingdom. Learning from those who govern well; be a guest of the king.
5: Contemplating your own life by looking at its effects on others. If those effects are good, you are on the right path and there is no blame.
6: Contemplating your own character, as if from the outside, to see whether it is truly noble. Honest self-examination, even when you are no longer at the center of things, keeps you from error.

@21 | Biting Through | Clarifying Justice
key: decisiveness; justice; obstacles; clarity; resolution
core: a time to bite through obstacles decisively and fairly, bringing clarity and justice to a stuck situation
now: Thunder and lightning: clarity combined with force. The image shows a mouth with something stuck between the teeth. Biting Through says an obstacle prevents things from coming together, and it must be dealt with energetically. The text favours "the use of legal constraints" — firm but just action. Whatever the obstacle is — a lie, a problem, a blocking person — it must be faced, understood and removed fairly.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a moment that requires decisive action. Obstacles will need to be faced clearly and justly.
do: Name the obstacle clearly; Act decisively but fairly; Set firm, appropriate consequences
avoid: Tolerating deceit or obstruction; Excessive harshness
love: If something is blocking closeness, address it directly and honestly.
work: Remove the bottleneck. Enforce rules fairly and clarify misunderstandings.
self: Face the habit or belief that keeps getting in the way.
jung: Biting Through may describe the need to confront something unpleasant that the ego would rather avoid — a shadow quality or an uncomfortable truth.
ex: Ending a toxic agreement, resolving a long-standing misunderstanding, or confronting dishonest behaviour.
1: Feet fastened in the stocks, toes hidden. A small punishment early prevents larger mistakes. No blame.
2: Biting through soft meat until the nose disappears. Reacting a little too strongly, but no blame.
3: Biting through old dried meat and meeting poison. A small humiliation, but no blame.
4: Biting through dried gristly meat and receiving metal arrows. Persist through difficulty. Good fortune.
5: Biting through dried lean meat and receiving yellow gold. Be firm and aware of danger. No blame.
6: Wearing a cangue that covers the ears. Refusing to listen leads to misfortune.

@22 | Grace | Adornment
key: beauty; form; presentation; culture; authenticity
core: a time when beauty and form matter, though grace must remain true to the substance underneath
now: Fire beneath the mountain lights up its beauty. Grace is about appearance, style and culture: the forms that make life pleasant and meaningful. The text says grace brings success in small matters. Beauty is valuable, but it is not enough for important decisions. Make sure the presentation is true to the substance. Elegance is at its best when simple.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a phase where presentation, beauty and form become important. Keep substance and appearance in harmony.
do: Present things clearly and attractively; Appreciate beauty and culture; Keep form true to substance
avoid: Superficial decisions based on appearances; Using style to hide flaws
love: Small gestures of beauty and care can delight, but deep connection needs more than appearance.
work: Present your work well, but do not let polish replace quality.
self: Express yourself creatively. Seek simple, authentic beauty.
jung: Grace may symbolize the persona, the social face we present to the world. Jung saw it as necessary but warned against identifying with it. The question is whether the outer form expresses the inner truth.
ex: Preparing a presentation, decorating a home, or choosing an outfit for an important occasion.
1: Gracing the toes, leaving the carriage and walking. Simplicity is better than a borrowed show.
2: Gracing the beard. Form follows substance; adornment depends on what it adorns.
3: Graceful and moist. Lasting firmness brings good fortune; do not get lost in comfort.
4: Grace or simplicity? A white horse comes as if on wings. A sincere partner, not an enemy, approaches.
5: Grace in the hills and gardens. A small, modest gift. Humiliation at first, then good fortune.
6: Simple grace. Natural simplicity brings no blame.

@23 | Splitting Apart | Stripping Away
key: decline; collapse; letting go; endurance; patience
core: a time of decline and stripping away, when it is wiser to wait than to push against collapse
now: A mountain rests on the earth, but its base is crumbling. Splitting Apart describes a time when structures are breaking down, often from below. The text says it does not further you to go anywhere. This is not the time for bold action. Protect what is essential, be generous to those who depend on you, and wait. Decay is part of the natural cycle; after it, renewal comes.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a phase of decline or loss. Protect what is essential and wait for renewal.
do: Protect your core values and resources; Accept what cannot be saved; Be generous to those who rely on you
avoid: Bold new undertakings; Fighting against inevitable change
love: If a relationship is weakening, focus on what can be preserved rather than blaming. Some things need time.
work: Avoid expansion. Protect essential resources and reputation.
self: Let go of what no longer serves you. Endings make room for beginnings.
jung: Splitting Apart can describe the breakdown of an old attitude or self-image. Painful as it is, such dissolution is often necessary before a new orientation can emerge from the unconscious.
ex: A business in decline, the end of a long relationship, or a period of declining health that calls for rest and acceptance.
1: The leg of the bed is split. Those who remain loyal are undermined. Misfortune.
2: The bed is split at the edge. The damage spreads. Misfortune.
3: Splitting apart from the bad elements. Breaking away from harmful company brings no blame.
4: The bed is split up to the skin. Danger is close. Misfortune.
5: A string of fishes; favour through the court ladies. Order is restored; everything furthers.
6: A large fruit uneaten. The superior person gets a carriage; the petty person loses the house. Goodness survives destruction.

@24 | Return | The Turning Point
key: return; renewal; turning point; recovery; natural cycles
core: a turning point, when the light returns and something healthy begins again quietly
now: Thunder within the earth: after the darkest point, the light returns. Return marks a turning point. One single solid line enters at the bottom, beneath five broken lines. The text says going out and coming in are without error, friends come, and on the seventh day comes return. Renewal begins quietly. Do not rush it. Rest, recover and let the new energy grow naturally. Returning to what is healthy, true and natural brings success.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a turning point and a fresh start. Let new energy grow gently, without forcing it.
do: Return to healthy habits and true values; Let renewal grow gently; Rest and recover
avoid: Rushing the new beginning; Falling back into old mistakes
love: A chance to reconnect or reconcile. Begin gently and rebuild trust.
work: A good moment to restart a project or return to core strengths.
self: Return to what nourishes you: rest, nature, honest reflection.
jung: Return may describe the moment when psychic energy, after a period of depression or withdrawal, begins to flow back into consciousness. Jung called the return of libido after introversion a renewal of life.
ex: Recovering after an illness, returning to an old passion, or reconciling after a falling-out.
1: Return from a short distance. No need for remorse. Great good fortune.
2: Quiet return. Good fortune.
3: Repeated return. Danger, but no blame.
4: Walking in the midst of others, returning alone. Following your own path back to what is right.
5: Noble-hearted return. No remorse.
6: Missing the return. Misfortune. Even a great effort will fail for a long time.
