@25 | Innocence | The Unexpected
key: innocence; sincerity; spontaneity; integrity; the unexpected
core: a time to act with sincerity and innocence, free of hidden motives, while accepting the unexpected
now: Thunder rolls under heaven, and all things are set in their true nature. Innocence describes acting naturally and sincerely, without calculation or hidden motives. The text promises supreme success to those who stay correct, but warns that anyone who is not as they should be will meet misfortune. Unexpected events may happen — some good, some not. The best response is to remain genuine, not to scheme. Act because it is right, not because of what you hope to gain.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a time when sincerity and spontaneity matter, and unexpected events test whether your motives are clear.
do: Act sincerely and honestly; Respond naturally to what happens; Do the right thing without calculating rewards
avoid: Manipulation and hidden motives; Rash actions based on wishful thinking
love: Be genuine and spontaneous; do not play games. Honest feeling is your strength.
work: Do good work for its own sake. Avoid schemes or shortcuts.
self: Reconnect with your natural, unguarded self.
jung: Innocence may suggest acting from the Self rather than the calculating ego. It can also point to the role of chance and the unexpected, the area where Jung located synchronicity.
ex: Helping someone without expecting thanks, or responding honestly to an unexpected turn of events.
1: Innocent behaviour brings good fortune. Acting from a pure heart, you succeed.
2: Do not count on the harvest while ploughing; do not calculate the yield while clearing the field. Do the work for its own sake, and it will further you.
3: Undeserved misfortune: a cow tied up by someone is taken by a passer-by, and the villagers are blamed. Sometimes trouble arrives without fault. Stay calm.
4: If you can remain firm and correct, there is no blame. Stay true to your nature.
5: Do not use medicine for an illness you did not cause. Some problems pass on their own; do not overreact.
6: Innocent action now brings misfortune. When the time is wrong, even sincere action fails. Wait.

@26 | The Taming Power of the Great | Great Accumulation
key: accumulation; restraint; learning; discipline; strength held in reserve
core: a time of great accumulation, when restraint and learning build strength that will later be released
now: Heaven within the mountain: great creative power is held, stored and nourished. This hexagram describes building up resources, knowledge and character through restraint and daily discipline. The text says it furthers you not to eat at home — that is, to take up public service — and to cross the great water. Hold your strength in check now, so that when you act, you act with great power. Study the words and deeds of the past to build your inner strength.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a phase of accumulation and discipline, when strength is gathered and held in reserve before being used.
do: Build up knowledge, skill and resources; Exercise self-discipline; Learn from history and experience
avoid: Releasing energy prematurely; Wasting accumulated strength
love: Commitment grows through patience and shared discipline. Build something lasting together.
work: Invest in skills and preparation. A larger opportunity will come.
self: Develop your character and inner strength through daily practice.
jung: Great Accumulation may suggest the containment of libido or psychic energy, holding it rather than discharging it at once, so that it can transform into something new.
ex: Training for a long race, studying for a qualification, or saving to start a business.
1: Danger is near. It is better to stop. Restraint now avoids harm.
2: The axle is removed from the cart. You cannot move now; accept the halt.
3: A good horse follows others. Practise your driving and defence daily; it furthers you to have somewhere to go.
4: The headboard of a young bull. Restraining a force before it grows dangerous. Great good fortune.
5: The tusk of a castrated boar. Restraining by removing the source of aggression. Good fortune.
6: The way of heaven is open. Strength is freed; success.

@27 | Nourishment | The Corners of the Mouth
key: nourishment; care; self-care; speech; what you consume
core: a time to examine what nourishes you and others, physically, emotionally and spiritually
now: Thunder beneath the mountain, like the movement of the jaw. Nourishment is about what sustains life — food, words, relationships and ideas. The text advises paying attention to what a person seeks to fill their mouth with: what you nourish, and how you nourish others, reveals your character. Be careful with your words and moderate in consumption. Nourish what is best in yourself and others.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a time when nourishment and care become central — what you feed yourself and others in body, mind and spirit.
do: Look at what you consume — food, media, relationships; Choose words carefully; Nourish others generously and wisely
avoid: Overindulgence; Envying what others have
love: Care for each other's wellbeing. Kind words nourish a relationship.
work: Look after your team's wellbeing. Invest in what truly sustains the organization.
self: Examine your habits: what feeds you and what drains you?
jung: Nourishment may point to the question of what feeds the psyche — which images, relationships and values sustain inner life. It can also suggest the mother archetype: care and dependence.
ex: Improving your diet, choosing healthier relationships, or caring for a sick family member.
1: You let go of your magic tortoise and look at me with your jaw drooping. Envying others' nourishment while ignoring your own resources brings misfortune.
2: Seeking nourishment from the wrong sources leads to misfortune. Rely on yourself where you can.
3: Turning away from true nourishment. For ten years do not act; nothing furthers.
4: Seeking nourishment from above to help others. Eager like a tiger, without blame.
5: Departing from the usual path. Stay firm; do not cross the great water.
6: The source of nourishment. Aware of the danger, you bring good fortune; it is right to cross the great water.

@28 | Preponderance of the Great | Critical Mass
key: excess; pressure; crisis; extraordinary measures; courage
core: a time of extraordinary pressure, like a beam about to give way, that calls for decisive but careful action
now: The lake rises above the trees: too much water. Preponderance of the Great describes a situation in which pressure is too much — the ridgepole of a house is sagging under the weight. The text says it furthers you to have somewhere to go: action is needed. Extraordinary times call for extraordinary measures, but they must be thoughtful and balanced. Do not panic, and do not freeze. Find a way to relieve the pressure.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a critical point where pressure becomes too great and decisive but careful action will be needed.
do: Recognize when pressure is too great; Act decisively to relieve it; Stay calm and independent in mind
avoid: Ignoring warning signs; Overreacting with drastic measures
love: The relationship may be under strain. Address the pressure honestly before something breaks.
work: You may be overloaded. Delegate, restructure or seek support.
self: You are carrying too much. Take steps to lighten the load.
jung: Preponderance of the Great can describe a situation where the conscious attitude has become one-sided, and pressure from the unconscious builds until something must give way.
ex: Being overwhelmed by work, caring for a sick relative alone, or a business facing a crisis.
1: Spreading white rushes beneath. Extreme care at the beginning. No blame.
2: A dry poplar sprouts at the root; an older man takes a young wife. Unexpected renewal is possible.
3: The ridgepole sags. Pushing ahead stubbornly leads to misfortune.
4: The ridgepole is braced. Good fortune, but ulterior motives bring humiliation.
5: A withered poplar puts forth flowers; an older woman takes a young husband. No blame, but no praise.
6: Wading too deep, the water goes over your head. Misfortune, but no blame.

@29 | The Abysmal | Water, Danger
key: danger; repetition; persistence; sincerity; flow
core: a time of repeated danger, which you pass through by staying sincere and moving steadily like flowing water
now: Water upon water: danger repeated. The Abysmal describes a situation in which difficulty follows difficulty. The text says that if you are sincere, you have success in your heart, and whatever you do succeeds. Water teaches how to deal with danger: it flows on, filling every hollow, never losing its nature. Do not panic or freeze. Hold to your sincerity and keep moving steadily, one step at a time.
dir: The situation may be moving toward repeated difficulties. Steadiness, sincerity and persistence will carry you through.
do: Stay calm and sincere; Take one careful step at a time; Learn from repeated experiences
avoid: Panic or giving up; Taking unnecessary risks
love: Stay honest and steady through difficulties. Shared hardship can deepen trust.
work: Navigate risk carefully. Keep a steady course and do not cut corners.
self: You may feel caught in recurring struggles. Look for the pattern and keep your inner sincerity.
jung: The Abysmal often symbolizes the unconscious itself, the dark water in which consciousness can drown or from which it can be renewed. It suggests facing fear rather than fleeing it.
ex: Going through a series of setbacks, coping with anxiety, or facing a difficult period with patience.
1: Repeated danger. Falling into a pit within the pit. Misfortune.
2: The pit is dangerous. Strive only for small gains.
3: Danger in front and behind. Do not act; wait.
4: A jug of wine, a bowl of rice, earthen vessels passed through a window. Simplicity and sincerity are enough. No blame.
5: The pit is not overflowing; it is filled only to the brim. No blame.
6: Bound with ropes and placed in a thicket of thorns. For three years you do not find the way. Misfortune.

@30 | The Clinging | Fire, Radiance
key: clarity; light; dependence; insight; connection
core: a time of clarity and brightness, which lasts when it is connected to something that sustains it
now: Fire upon fire: brightness doubled. The Clinging describes light, clarity and awareness. Fire must cling to something in order to burn — wood, oil, a wick. Likewise, clarity depends on something to sustain it. The text promises success to firm correctness and advises caring for the cow — a symbol of docility and nourishment. Recognize what you depend on, and let your understanding be warm and steady, not frantic.
dir: The situation may be moving toward greater clarity and insight. It will be important to recognize what you depend on and to keep your light steady.
do: Seek clarity and understanding; Recognize what sustains you; Share insight warmly
avoid: Burning out from too much intensity; Depending on unstable sources
love: Bring warmth and understanding into the relationship. Clarify feelings honestly.
work: Clarity and vision are your strengths. Communicate them clearly.
self: Shed light on your situation. Identify what you truly depend on.
jung: The Clinging may symbolize consciousness itself — the light of awareness that depends on its connection to the unconscious for its fuel.
ex: Gaining insight into a problem, a teacher inspiring students, or realizing how much you depend on a relationship.
1: The footprints run crisscross. With respect and care, there is no blame.
2: Yellow light. The middle way brings supreme good fortune.
3: In the light of the setting sun, people either beat the pot and sing or lament old age. Accept the passing of time.
4: Its coming is sudden; it flames up, dies down, and is thrown away. Sudden flare-ups burn out quickly.
5: Tears in floods, sighing and lamenting. Sincere remorse brings good fortune.
6: The king goes forth to punish. Correct the leaders of wrongdoing, not the followers.
