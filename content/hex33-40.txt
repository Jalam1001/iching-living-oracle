@33 | Retreat | Withdrawal
key: retreat; withdrawal; distance; self-preservation; timing
core: a time to step back from an unfavourable situation, withdrawing with dignity to preserve your strength
now: Mountain beneath heaven: the mountain rises, but heaven withdraws before it. Retreat describes a time when opposing forces are growing stronger and continued engagement would waste your energy. The traditional text says that success lies in withdrawal, and that only in small matters is it still useful to persist. Retreat is not defeat. It is a deliberate choice to create distance, keep your dignity and preserve your strength for a better moment. The Image adds an important detail: the wise person keeps petty people at a distance, not with hatred, but with calm reserve.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a time when stepping back is wiser than pushing on. Creating distance, without bitterness, can protect your energy and independence.
do: Recognize when continued effort is no longer productive; Withdraw calmly and without resentment; Keep your dignity and your options open
avoid: Staying too long out of pride or habit; Leaving in anger or creating enemies
love: If a relationship is draining or one-sided, some distance may help both people see clearly. Step back with kindness, not punishment.
work: A project, role or negotiation may no longer be worth the cost. Plan an orderly exit or reduce your involvement without burning bridges.
self: Protect your energy. Saying no to some things is a way of saying yes to yourself.
jung: Psychologically, Retreat may describe the healthy withdrawal of energy from an outer situation that no longer holds meaning, so that it can turn inward and be renewed. Jung saw such periods of introversion as a natural phase of growth, not a failure.
ex: Leaving a job with a toxic culture, stepping back from a family argument, or ending a commitment that has become an obligation without purpose.
1: The tail end of a retreat: you are the last to leave and the most exposed. This is dangerous. Do not start anything new now; simply get out of harm's way quietly.
2: Held fast as if by a strap of yellow oxhide that cannot be broken. A firm commitment or loyalty holds you in place. Here, staying true to what binds you is right, even while others withdraw.
3: A retreat that is held back by ties and obligations, which is stressful and dangerous. Treat the people who hold you back kindly, as you would care for those who depend on you, and the situation can turn out well.
4: Voluntary retreat, even from something you like. A mature person can let go of what they enjoy when the time is right and finds good fortune in it. Someone ruled by desire cannot.
5: A friendly, well-timed retreat. Leaving at the right moment, with courtesy and firm purpose, brings good fortune.
6: A cheerful, free retreat. You leave without regret, resentment or entanglement, and everything works out well.

@34 | The Power of the Great | Great Strength
key: power; strength; momentum; restraint; integrity
core: a time of great strength and momentum, which succeeds only when power is guided by what is right
now: Thunder high in heaven: strength in motion. The Power of the Great describes a time when you have energy, confidence and influence. The text says firm correctness is what makes this favourable. Real strength includes self-control. The Image advises not taking any step that is not in accordance with what is proper. Power without restraint can run into walls, like a ram that gets its horns stuck in a fence. Use your strength for what is right and know when not to push.
dir: The situation may be moving toward greater strength and momentum. The challenge will be to use power wisely and with restraint.
do: Use your strength for good purposes; Combine power with self-control; Pause before forcing an outcome
avoid: Aggression or bullying; Pushing headlong into obstacles
love: Your strength can be protective and reassuring, but make sure it does not become controlling.
work: You have influence and momentum. Use it fairly, and do not overreach.
self: Feel your strength, then choose to direct it carefully.
jung: The Power of the Great may describe a surge of energy from the unconscious that empowers the ego. The risk is identification with power, an inflation that loses sight of limits.
ex: Getting a big promotion, winning an argument, or feeling unusually energetic and confident.
1: Strength in the toes: pushing forward from the very bottom with no real position. Advancing now leads to misfortune. Wait.
2: Firm correctness brings good fortune. Your strength is well balanced; keep it that way.
3: A petty person uses force; a superior person does not, knowing it is dangerous. A ram that butts a fence gets its horns caught. Do not force things.
4: Firm correctness brings good fortune and regret vanishes. The fence opens and the horns are free. Strength is like the axle of a big cart: steady, quiet power that carries you forward.
5: Losing the ram's stubbornness with ease. Letting go of the urge to force brings no regret.
6: A ram butts the fence and cannot go forward or back. Nothing furthers. Recognizing the difficulty brings good fortune in the end.

love: The relationship can grow openly and joyfully. Share your progress with your partner.
work: A good time for advancement, visibility and recognition.
self: Let your best qualities shine. Growth comes naturally when you are clear about who you are.
jung: Progress, the rising sun, may symbolize expanding consciousness. As awareness grows, the ego can make progress, provided it remains connected to the deeper Self.
ex: Getting a promotion, receiving public recognition, or making steady progress in learning.
1: Progressing, but held back. Stay correct and calm. If others do not yet trust you, stay generous and relaxed; there is no mistake.
2: Progressing, but feeling sorrowful. Stay firm. Great blessing will come from a motherly or caring figure.
3: Everyone agrees with you. Support from others makes regret disappear.
4: Progressing like a squirrel: clinging to position and grasping. This is dangerous even if correct.
5: Regret disappears. Do not worry about gain or loss; going forward brings good fortune.

Darkness does not last forever.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a difficult or unappreciative environment, where discretion and inner steadiness protect you.
do: Protect your integrity quietly; Be discreet and do not provoke; Keep your inner light alive
avoid: Openly fighting a hostile environment; Losing hope
love: Be patient through difficult times. Protect what is precious between you.
work: In a hostile or unfair environment, keep a low profile and protect your reputation.
self: Hold on to your inner values even when they are not reflected around you.
jung: Darkening of the Light may describe a time when consciousness is overwhelmed by the shadow, depression or outer difficulties. The task is to keep a spark of awareness alive through the darkness.
ex: Working under an unfair boss, living through a period of depression, or enduring a time of injustice.
1: Darkening of the light while flying: the wings droop. You may have to go without comfort for a while. People may misunderstand you, but persevere.
2: Wounded in the left thigh. Help comes from a strong horse. Use whatever support you can; there is good fortune.
3: During a hunt in the south, the leader of darkness is captured. Change cannot be rushed, but it is now possible.
4: Entering the left side of the belly, understanding the heart of the darkening, and then leaving through the gate. Knowing the true situation, you withdraw.
5: Prince Ji's darkening of the light: he hid his wisdom to survive under a tyrant. Firmness furthers.
6: Not light but darkness. First it climbs to heaven, then it plunges into the earth. Darkness destroys itself.

@37 | The Family | The Clan
key: family; roles; home; responsibility; loyalty
core: a time to strengthen close relationships and the home, where clear roles and sincere words create order
now: Wind comes forth from fire: influence spreads outward from the hearth. The Family describes the inner circle of life — home, family, close relationships — and the clear roles and mutual respect that make it work. The text says it is advantageous for the woman of the household to be firm and correct. In modern terms, the hexagram emphasizes responsibility, consistency and sincere words that are backed by actions. When the family is in order, the wider world is set in order too.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a focus on home, family and close relationships, where clear roles and loving discipline are needed.
do: Make your words match your actions; Clarify roles and responsibilities; Show warmth and loyalty
avoid: Inconsistency; Harshness or neglect at home
love: Commitment and shared responsibilities strengthen the relationship. Be reliable.
work: Treat the team like a family: clear roles, mutual respect and consistency.
self: Look at the patterns you learned at home. Which ones support you?
jung: The Family may point to the family complexes that shape the personality. It invites reflection on the roles and patterns inherited from parents and their effects on present relationships.
ex: Setting new household routines, resolving family tensions, or building trust in a small team.
1: Firm boundaries within the family. Clear rules from the start prevent later regret.
2: She does not follow her whims, but attends to the household. Steady care brings good fortune.
3: When family members are treated too harshly, there is regret, but still good fortune; when they giggle and jest without restraint, humiliation follows. Strictness is better than indulgence.
4: She is the treasure of the house. Great good fortune.
5: As a king approaches his family, there is no fear. Good fortune. Lead with love.
6: Sincerity and dignity bring good fortune in the end.

love: You may see things differently. Small gestures of understanding can bridge the gap.

1: Regret disappears. If you lose your horse, do not run after it; it will return by itself. When you meet unpleasant people, be courteous.

work: A project may hit obstacles. Regroup, seek advice and change your approach.

2: The king's servant meets obstacle after obstacle, but not through his own fault. Keep working for the greater good.
3: Going leads to obstruction, so he comes back. Return to those who need you.
4: Going leads to obstruction; coming back brings union. Gather allies before acting.
