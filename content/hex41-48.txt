@41 | Decrease | Reduction
key: decrease; simplicity; sacrifice; restraint; sincerity
core: a time of decrease, when reducing excess, simplifying and giving sincerely can strengthen what matters most
now: A lake at the foot of a mountain: the lake gives up water to the mountain, which becomes greener. Decrease describes a time when something must be reduced, given up or simplified. The text says that if decrease is combined with sincerity, it brings great good fortune, and that even two small bowls are enough for a sacrifice. Less can be more. Cutting back on excess, controlling anger and desire, and giving to what deserves support can strengthen what is essential.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a period of reduction or simplification. Sincerity and wise sacrifice will make it fruitful.
do: Simplify and reduce excess; Give sincerely where it helps; Control anger and impulses
avoid: Resentment about loss; Reducing what is essential
love: Let go of unnecessary demands or expectations. Simple sincerity is enough.
work: Cut costs or simplify processes. Focus resources where they matter most.
self: Practise restraint and simplicity. Ask what you can let go of.
jung: Decrease may describe the reduction of ego-demands so that something deeper can grow. Sacrificing a one-sided attitude can make space for the Self.
ex: Reducing spending, decluttering, cutting back on commitments, or giving time to others.
1: Going quickly when your own tasks are done brings no blame, but consider how much you can reduce yourself to help others.
2: It furthers you to stay firm; acting now would be unfortunate. You can increase others without decreasing yourself.
3: When three people travel together, one is lost; when one travels alone, he finds a companion. Groups of three tend to split into pairs.
4: Decreasing your faults makes the other person hasten to you with joy. No blame.
5: Someone increases you with ten pairs of tortoise shells, a gift that cannot be refused. Supreme good fortune.
6: Increasing others without decreasing yourself. No blame. Firmness brings good fortune; you gain helpers who serve the common good.
