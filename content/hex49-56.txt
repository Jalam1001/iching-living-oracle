@49 | Revolution | Radical Change
key: change; transformation; timing; renewal; conviction
core: a time of fundamental change, which succeeds when the moment is right and people trust the reason for it
now: Fire within the lake: two forces that cannot both stay as they are. Revolution describes a deep transformation, like an animal shedding its old coat. The traditional text says that people will believe in the change only "on the day of its completion", and that firm correctness makes regret disappear. Real change needs the right timing, a clear and fair purpose, and the trust of others. The Image advises the wise person to set the calendar in order and make the seasons clear — understand the rhythm of time before you overturn what exists.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a fundamental change. When the time is right and your motives are clear, transformation can bring renewal.
do: Make sure change is truly necessary and well-timed; Explain your reasons so others can trust them; Change what is outdated, keep what is sound
avoid: Changing things for the sake of change; Acting before you have support or clarity
love: The relationship may need to change its old patterns. Talk openly about what must be different, and change together.
work: Outdated structures or methods may need to be replaced. Prepare carefully and communicate clearly.
self: You may be ready to shed an old identity or habit. Let the new you emerge at a natural pace.
jung: Revolution can describe a fundamental shift in attitude, the kind of transformation Jung associated with the process of individuation, when an old way of being no longer fits and a new orientation emerges.
ex: Changing careers, ending a habit that defined you, or transforming how a team or family works.
1: Wrapped in the hide of a yellow ox. It is too early to act. Hold firm and let the time for change mature.
2: When the day comes, carry out the change. The time is now right; going forward brings good fortune and no blame.
3: Charging ahead brings misfortune, and stubbornness is dangerous. Only when talk of change has been considered three times does it deserve trust. Check your plans carefully.
4: Regret disappears. People trust you, and changing the old order now brings good fortune. Sincerity gives the change its legitimacy.
5: The great person changes like a tiger, whose stripes are clear and bright. The change is so evident that no oracle is needed; people trust it.
6: The superior person changes like a leopard, the petty person changes only their face. After the main change, do not keep pushing. Settling down and staying firm brings good fortune.

@50 | The Cauldron | Nourishing Transformation
key: transformation; nourishment; culture; refinement; value
core: a time of refinement and nourishing transformation, when raw materials become something of lasting value
now: Fire over wood: a sacred bronze cauldron in which food is cooked and offerings are prepared. The Cauldron is one of the few hexagrams that represents a man-made object, and it stands for culture, refinement and spiritual nourishment. The text promises supreme good fortune and success. Like cooking, transformation needs the right vessel, the right ingredients and the right heat. Put your gifts to good use and serve something larger than yourself.
dir: The situation may be moving toward refinement and transformation, where raw potential becomes something nourishing and valuable.
do: Turn raw ideas or talents into something of value; Serve a purpose larger than yourself; Respect tradition while renewing it
avoid: Wasting talents; Carrying more than the vessel can hold
love: Nourish the relationship with care, shared values and meaningful rituals.
work: Refine products, skills or teams into something of lasting value.
self: Your talents are being refined. Use them to nourish others.
jung: The Cauldron resembles the alchemical vessel, a symbol Jung used for the container in which psychological transformation happens. Raw emotion and experience are "cooked" into insight.
ex: Developing a skill into a craft, preparing a meaningful meal or ceremony, or transforming experience into wisdom.
1: The cauldron is turned upside down to empty out old residue. Clearing away what is spoiled is helpful, even if it seems unusual. No blame.
2: The cauldron has food in it. Others may envy you, but they cannot harm you. Keep your substance and stay focused. Good fortune.
3: The handle of the cauldron is changed, so it cannot be lifted, and the good food is not eaten. Your gifts are not being used. Wait; when the rain comes, regret disappears and good fortune follows.
4: The cauldron's leg breaks and the prince's meal is spilled. Taking on more than you can handle leads to failure and embarrassment.
5: The cauldron has yellow handles and golden carrying rings. You are open to help and good advice. Firmness brings benefit.
6: The cauldron has rings of jade. Great good fortune; everything is beneficial. Wisdom and gentleness are in harmony.

@51 | The Arousing | Shock, Thunder

core: a sudden shock or upheaval that frightens at first but can awaken you and lead to composure and growth
now: Thunder repeated: a sudden shock. The Arousing describes events that startle and shake you. Shock can awaken you. The Image advises reflecting on yourself and setting your life in order. Fear passes; what remains is the lesson.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a shock or sudden change. Stay composed; it can awaken you to something important.
do: Stay calm when shocks occur; Use the shock to examine and correct yourself; Return to stability after the surprise
avoid: Panic; Ignoring the lesson of a shock
love: An unexpected event may shake the relationship. Stay steady and talk through what happened.
work: Sudden changes may occur. Keep composure and adapt quickly.
self: A shock can wake you up. Ask what it is showing you.
jung: The Arousing may describe a sudden irruption from the unconscious — an event, dream or emotion that shakes the ego out of complacency and opens it to change.
ex: An unexpected job loss, a health scare, or sudden news that changes your perspective.
1: Shock comes with fear and trembling, then laughter and relief. Good fortune follows the scare.
2: Shock brings danger and you lose your treasures. Climb to higher ground and do not chase what is lost; in seven days it will return.
3: Shock leaves you distraught. If shock moves you to act correctly, there is no harm.
4: Shock leaves you stuck in the mud. Your energy is bogged down; you cannot respond well right now.
5: Shocks come and go, and danger surrounds you. Do not lose your center; there is still work to be done.
6: Shock causes panic and wild looks. Going forward now brings misfortune. If the shock has not yet hit you but your neighbour, you can avoid harm. There may be gossip.

@52 | Keeping Still | Mountain

The Image advises the wise person not to let thoughts go beyond their situation. Stop when it is time to stop, move when it is time to move, and let the mind settle.
dir: The situation may be moving toward a time of stillness. Calm reflection and knowing when to stop will matter more than action.

avoid: Restlessness; Overthinking beyond what you can affect
love: Give each other space and quiet. Stillness can bring clarity.
work: Pause before big decisions. Rest prevents burnout.
self: A perfect time for meditation, retreat or rest.
jung: Keeping Still resembles meditation and the quieting of the ego so that the deeper Self can be perceived. Stillness allows unconscious contents to surface without being disturbed.
ex: Taking a retreat, pausing a project to reflect, or learning to calm an anxious mind.
1: Keeping the toes still. Stopping before you even start prevents mistakes. Lasting firmness is beneficial.
2: Keeping the calves still. You cannot stop the person you follow, and your heart is not glad. You may have to go along with something you disagree with.
3: Keeping the hips still and stiffening the back. Forcing stillness creates tension and danger. True calm cannot be forced.
4: Keeping the trunk still. You have calmed yourself, even if you cannot yet calm others. No blame.
5: Keeping the jaws still. Speaking carefully and in order makes regret disappear.
6: Noble-hearted stillness. A deep, settled calm brings good fortune.

dir: The situation may be moving toward a role where you have limited power. Patience, tact and dignity will be essential.

work: You may be in a junior or dependent position. Work with dignity and make yourself valuable.
self: Notice where you feel subordinate or dependent. Accept limits while keeping self-respect.
jung: The Marrying Maiden may describe a relationship in which one person carries a projection that does not truly fit them. Psychological maturity requires recognizing the real person behind the projection.
ex: Starting in a junior role, joining a family as an in-law, or accepting a partnership on unequal terms.
1: The marrying maiden as a secondary wife. A lame person who can still walk. Advancing brings good fortune.
2: A one-eyed person who can still see. Steadfastness of a solitary person furthers.
3: The marrying maiden as a slave. She marries as a concubine. Accepting a degrading role is not wise.
4: The marrying maiden delays the wedding. A late marriage comes in its time.
5: The sovereign gave his daughter in marriage. Her sleeves were less splendid than those of the bridesmaid. The moon nearly full. Good fortune.
6: The woman holds a basket, but there is no fruit. The man stabs the sheep, but no blood flows. Nothing furthers.
